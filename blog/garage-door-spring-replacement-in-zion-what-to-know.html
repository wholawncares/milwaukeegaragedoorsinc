Garage door springs: what Zion homeowners should know

Springs carry the weight of your garage door, and they fail by design after a set number of cycles. Here is how they work, how to tell when one breaks, and what national guides say about replacement.

Milwaukee Garage Doors IncNovember 12, 20269 min read

Garage door springs do the heavy lifting. They counterbalance the door's weight so the opener, and your hands, move a load that would otherwise be far too heavy. They are also wear items: every open-and-close cycle uses up a small part of their life, and they eventually break.

This guide explains what springs do, how to recognize a broken one, the difference between torsion and extension springs, how long springs last according to national guides, and what national guide ranges say about replacement costs. Dollar figures are labeled national guide ranges, not quotes and not this business's price.

What a garage door spring actually does

A garage door is heavy, and its springs store energy as the door closes and release it as the door opens, counterbalancing the weight through the travel. Without working springs, the full weight falls on the opener and on anyone lifting the door by hand. That is why a door with a broken spring feels extremely heavy and why the opener strains or gives up.

Springs work with cables, drums, and the opener as one system. The springs provide the lift, the cables carry the load to the door, and the opener controls the movement. A failure in one part loads the others, which is why a broken spring can lead to cable and opener damage if the door keeps being used.

Because springs hold the door's weight under high tension, all spring work is technician work. Adjusting, replacing, or releasing a spring without the right tools and training can release that energy suddenly. This guide explains springs; it does not instruct you to work on them.

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken in Zion Illinois?

The signs are usually unmistakable, and customers describe them in the same words again and again:

  • A loud bang or pop from the garage, then the door will not lift. Guides describe the sound as like a firecracker. It is the strongest spring-break indicator.
  • A visible gap in the spring coil. A broken torsion spring often shows a clear gap where the coil has separated. Look, do not touch.
  • The door feels extremely heavy or impossible to lift by hand. With the opener disconnected, a door with a broken spring will not stay open and drops fast. Do not force it.
  • The door opens a few inches and stops, or closes then reopens. That symptom has more than one cause, including sensors and opener force settings, but a spring or balance problem is a common one. The opener side is covered in garage door opener repair in Zion, IL.
  • The door slams shut, falls fast, or will not stay open. That is a balance failure, and it is safety-sensitive. Stop using the door.
  • The door hangs crooked or sags on one side. That can mean a cable failure alongside or instead of a spring failure. See garage door off-track repair in Zion, IL.

If you see any of these signs, stop using the door and the opener. Each additional cycle loads the opener and cables with weight they were not designed to carry alone.

Torsion vs. extension springs: which one is on your door

Torsion springs mount horizontally above the closed door on a shaft, and twist to store energy. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks on either side of the door, and stretch as the door closes. You can usually tell which system you have by looking: a shaft and coil or coils above the door means torsion; long springs parallel to the tracks mean extension.

The guides agree that torsion springs last longer than extension springs, and that springs should be replaced in pairs or sets. Lifespans differ between the guides and both are reported in the FAQ section below, with sources and dates, rather than merged into one figure.

Extension systems are associated in the guides with older homes and single-panel tilt-up doors, and torsion systems with sectional doors. A technician can identify the system on sight and size replacements by door weight.

Conversion from extension to torsion is a defined job in the guides, with the national guide range given in the cost section above. Whether conversion suits a particular door is a technician's judgement based on the door, the headroom, and the hardware.

What spring replacement involves

No time promise is made here. What the work involves, in general terms, is: identifying the spring system and sizing replacements by door weight; safely releasing and handling the tensioned parts; replacing the springs as a set; checking cables, drums, bearings, and hardware in the same system; restoring balance; and testing full travel with the safety reverse and sensors.

Replacing springs in pairs or sets matters because both springs in a pair have the same cycle count. When one breaks, the other has lived the same life. Both guides that state lifespans agree on set replacement, and the cost figures above are mostly set figures for that reason.

After spring work, the door should lift without unusual heaviness, stay where it is left partway up when disconnected from the opener, and run full travel smoothly. If it does not, the balance or another part of the system needs attention before the door returns to regular use.

Can I replace a garage door spring myself in Zion?

No. Spring replacement is technician work, and this guide does not provide instructions for it. Springs hold the door's weight under high tension, and the tools and procedures for releasing and restoring that tension safely are specialised. A mistake can release the stored energy suddenly and cause serious injury.

What you can do yourself is recognize the signs, stop using the door, and call a technician. Do not try to unwind, adjust, or remove a spring, and do not prop the door open and keep using it. If a vehicle is trapped inside, say so when you call.

The same technician-only framing applies to cables, which work with the springs under the same tension. If a cable looks frayed, loose, or off its drum, leave it alone and call for garage door off-track repair in Zion, IL.

Common mistakes on spring jobs in Zion

  1. Replacing one spring instead of the set. The surviving spring has the same cycle count and is the next failure. Guides agree on set replacement.
  2. Running the opener on a broken spring. The opener carries weight it was not designed for, which can damage the drive and snap a cable.
  3. Guessing the spring size. Springs are sized by door weight. The wrong spring leaves the door unbalanced, heavy, or over-lifted. Sizing is part of the technician's job.
  4. Ignoring the cables and hardware. Springs, cables, drums, and bearings wear together. A spring job should include a look at the rest of the system.
  5. Skipping the balance and safety tests. After spring work, balance, full travel, auto-reverse, and sensors should all be checked before the door returns to regular use.

When to call a professional

Call a professional as soon as you hear a bang from the garage, see a gap in a spring coil, find the door extremely heavy or unable to stay open, or notice the door dropping fast or hanging crooked. Stop using the door and the opener until it has been seen.

How long do garage door springs last in Zion IL?

National guides differ and both are reported: HomeGuide (updated October 27, 2025) gives torsion 7-14 years or 10,000-20,000 cycles and extension 4-10 years or 5,000-15,000 cycles, while This Old House (updated March 10, 2026) gives torsion 8-15 years or up to 20,000 cycles and extension 7-12 years or about 10,000 cycles. Both agree springs should be replaced in sets and torsion lasts longer.

Should both springs be replaced at once?

Yes, according to both national guides that state lifespans. Springs in a pair share the same cycle count, so when one breaks the other has lived the same life. Set replacement is the standard the guides describe, and most guide cost figures are set figures for that reason.
